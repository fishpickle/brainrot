# Evaluation Findings — 2026-07-27

## Evaluation Summary

**Prompt version:** v1
**Model:** GPT-5.6 Terra
**Evaluation set:** 24 prompts (12 Standard→Gen Alpha, 12 Gen Alpha→Standard)
**Date:** 2026-07-27

### Overall assessment

The translator reliably preserves meaning but currently behaves more like a
paraphraser with slang substitutions than a true register translator. The
primary opportunity is improving intensity differentiation while maintaining
semantic fidelity.

## Key Findings

### 1. The intensity levels are not reliably differentiated

Mild, Cooked, and Ohio Max often share the same sentence structure and core
wording. Higher levels frequently add a single slang marker rather than
producing a meaningfully stronger change in register, rhythm, or style. A few
Ohio Max outputs demonstrate the desired creativity, but this behavior is not
consistent.

### 2. The prompt is collapsing around a small slang vocabulary

“No cap” appears as a default intensifier across unrelated contexts. Other
recurring constructions include “gotta,” “lock in,” and “slide into.” These
phrases can be appropriate, but their repeated use makes the translator feel
like a phrase-substitution tool rather than a context-aware translation
product.

### 3. Meaning preservation is generally strong, but tone sometimes drifts

Most outputs retain the central proposition, especially in the Gen Alpha to
Standard English direction. The weaker cases preserve the topic while changing
the social intent, emotional tone, or implied meaning. Creative outputs can
also introduce concepts that were not present in the source.

### 4. Reverse translation is reliable but intensity is under-defined

Gen Alpha to Standard English outputs consistently recover the basic meaning.
However, the three intensity settings are often identical in this direction.
This suggests that the product lacks a clear definition of what intensity
should control when the target is Standard English.

## Supporting Evidence

- “Dinner is ready” produced the same “Dinner’s ready, no cap” output for Mild
  and Cooked. Ohio Max changed more, but “come get this W” introduced a
  competitive tone absent from the source.
- “Please email me tomorrow morning” changed only modestly across all three
  levels. Cooked and Ohio Max both used “slide into” and “no cap,” with little
  increase in intensity.
- All three meeting outputs were effectively “The meeting got pushed back,”
  with “no cap” appended at higher levels.
- The chemistry Cooked and Ohio Max outputs were nearly identical, showing
  little separation beyond Mild.
- “My mom said I have to clean my room” produced a clearly distinct Ohio Max
  result using “de-cringe.” This shows the model can create stronger stylistic
  differentiation, although “de-cringe” also shifts the literal meaning of
  “clean.”
- The mitochondria outputs showed one of the strongest intensity progressions.
  The Ohio Max result was more playful while retaining the cellular-energy
  concept.
- “Bro is absolutely cooked” and “Touch grass” were translated accurately into
  Standard English, but Mild, Cooked, and Ohio Max were mostly identical.
- “Touch grass” became “Go outside and get some fresh air.” The literal action
  was retained, but the original criticism of being overly online or out of
  touch was softened.

## Recommended Prompt Changes

### 1. Define each intensity as a distinct transformation contract

Give every level observable requirements. Mild should make a small number of
natural substitutions. Cooked should recast rhythm or syntax and use multiple
contextual transformations. Ohio Max should be highly stylized and inventive
while preserving every factual claim and the source intent. Explicitly require
each level to be materially distinguishable from the level below.

### 2. Prohibit decorative slang and require contextual relevance

Instruct the model not to append generic slang merely to signal intensity.
Terms such as “no cap” should appear only when they contribute sincerity,
truthfulness, disbelief, or appropriate emphasis. Every slang choice should
serve the source meaning, tone, or communicative intent.

### 3. Establish a fidelity hierarchy

Require preservation in this order: factual meaning, communicative intent,
emotional and interpersonal tone, then style. Creativity should operate within
those constraints. This would reduce cases where an entertaining phrase adds a
new attitude or concept.

### 4. Add direction-specific intensity guidance

Define what intensity changes in Gen Alpha to Standard English. For example,
Mild could retain some slang flavor, Cooked could produce natural conversational
English, and Ohio Max could fully unpack implied meaning and attitude. If that
is not the intended product behavior, intensity should not influence reverse
translation.

## Recommended UX Changes

- Clarify intensity with short behavioral descriptions rather than relying on
  the labels alone.
- Reconsider whether the intensity control belongs in Gen Alpha to Standard
  English mode. A direction-specific control such as Literal, Plain, or
  Explained may be more understandable.
- Set expectations that Ohio Max is more stylized and may involve greater tone
  transformation, while still promising meaning preservation.
- Make the explanations surface meaning-sensitive changes and introduced
  stylistic choices, not just word substitutions.

## Next Experiments

1. Compare the current prompt with a version containing explicit intensity
   contracts and an anti-filler rule.
2. Run each input and intensity combination multiple times to measure repeated
   slang, adjacent-level similarity, and output stability.
3. Score meaning fidelity, tone fidelity, naturalness, intensity fit, and
   originality separately instead of using only a keep/reject judgment.
4. Expand the dataset with questions, requests, negation, emotion, sarcasm,
   formality, profanity, ambiguity, and multi-sentence inputs.
5. Balance both translation directions and test a direction-specific intensity
   definition against a version with no reverse-direction intensity.
6. Review intensity triplets side by side and record whether Cooked is
   materially stronger than Mild and Ohio Max is materially stronger than
   Cooked.

## Success Criteria for Next Iteration

The next prompt revision will be considered successful if:

- Ohio Max is consistently distinguishable from Cooked.
- Cooked is consistently distinguishable from Mild.
- Repetitive filler phrases (e.g. "no cap") appear significantly less often.
- Meaning fidelity remains high.
- Tone drift does not increase.
