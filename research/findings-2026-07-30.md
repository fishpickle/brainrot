# Evaluation Findings — 2026-07-30

## Evaluation Summary

**Prompt versions:** v5.1 and v6

**Model:** GPT-5.6 Terra

**Dataset:** 15 held-out Standard→Gen Alpha inputs across idioms, everyday
language, technical content, pragmatic sensitivity, and coinage opportunities

**Runs:** Three per input and prompt; 90 total

**Rater:** AI product review using the split rubric in
`prompt-v6-held-out-eval.md`. These ratings are an initial assessment and can
be overridden by the product owner without changing the raw generations.

### Overall result

The initial AI review understated Prompt v6's product fit. After product-owner
calibration of the disputed translations, v6 has the stronger translation
result: 33/45 (73%) versus 27/45 (60%) for v5.1. It substantially
reduces under-translation and improves recoverable coinage while supporting the
intentionally stylized, performative register.

V6 is now a promising successor candidate, but it should not become the
default solely from this evaluation. Explanation alignment remains weaker,
several undisputed meaning and naturalness failures remain, and the current
suite has now become a regression set rather than an unbiased promotion set.

| Measure | v5.1 | v6 |
|---|---:|---:|
| AI translation approval | 27/45 (60%) | 25/45 (56%) |
| Product-owner-adjusted translation approval | 27/45 (60%) | 33/45 (73%) |
| Explanation alignment | 40/45 (89%) | 33/45 (73%) |
| AI: not transformed enough | 12 | 4 |
| AI: meaning changed | 1 | 6 |

The failure counts retain the original AI labels for auditability. Product

## Key Findings

### 1. The transformation floor works

V6 reduces under-translation failures from twelve to four. Its largest gains
occur when an existing idiom or concrete organization task supports a
transparent recast:

- “threw me under the bus” → “bus-yeeted me” passed all three runs.
- “organized” → “cable-maxxed” and “cable-Tetris’d” produced recoverable,
  context-specific inventions.
- “nobody understands it anymore” became coherent spreadsheet lore, boss-fight,
  and maze systems.

These results validate high-leverage transformation and rules-based coinage as
product strategies.

### 2. Expressive expansion is closer to the product vision than the initial
rating suggested

The product owner approved all three D2 outputs, including “crashout-coded,”
“patch-noted the plan,” and “low-key crashout.” In this deliberately
performative translator, transparent metaphor and slight emotional
amplification can be desirable even when the wording is denser than ordinary
speech.

This does not make expressive expansion unlimited. The two remaining
pragmatic-sensitivity failures and the undisputed failures elsewhere still
show that polarity, speech act, central facts, and recognizable intent need
firm protection. The correct distinction is not literal versus figurative; it
is recoverable, entertaining expansion versus a new claim or emotional state.

### 3. First-letter profanity redaction is an accepted translation behavior

The product owner approved all three D3 translations and their printer-failure
metaphors: “crash-quit,” “rage-quit,” and “hard-DC’d.” The use of `f-` in the
translation is also approved when omission would flatten the source tone.

The behavioral specification now permits either a non-profane substitute or
first-letter redaction, while continuing to prohibit unredacted profanity.
The original AI failure labels remain in the artifact as evidence of the
pre-calibration rubric. The product owner found no issue with the explanation
ratings, so their aggregate result remains unchanged.

### 4. Explanations rationalize translation drift

V6 explanations are aligned in 73% of runs versus 89% for v5.1. Its twelve
failures are evenly split between semantically mismatched spans and inaccurate
reasons.

When v6 changes meaning, the explanation often claims that the source was
preserved. This makes the explanation regression more consequential than broad
span selection: it can conceal a translation defect rather than merely
describe it imprecisely.

### 5. Technical performance remains mixed

Both prompts handle deployment rollback and catalyst behavior relatively well.
V6 fails the museum-acquisition input in all three runs by using “secured,” an
ordinary synonym, while one explanation incorrectly calls it online,
status-conscious language.

The evidence does not support further technical-specific prompt growth before
the larger forward strategy is simplified.

### 6. Vocabulary diversity regresses despite more invention

V6 repeatedly falls back to generic markers across unrelated inputs:

- `yeet` or `yeeted`: seven outputs
- `low-key`: seven outputs
- `no cap`: four outputs

`No cap` is particularly weak when appended to responsibility or delivery
statements. V6 creates more novel compounds, but it also exhibits stronger
phrasebook behavior around a small set of familiar markers.

### 7. Target-register naturalness permits controlled grammatical strain

The product owner approved “two days early-bird from the expected drop” and
“a view that goes giga.” Both strain Standard English, but remain
understandable, original, and plausible within a playful tween register.

This differs from rejected constructions such as “Your turn got the ball,”
“forest-exited,” and “seat-save me.” Those feel mechanically assembled rather
than culturally or expressively motivated. Naturalness should therefore test
target-register plausibility and recoverability, not conformity to polished
Standard English.

The “respawned out the forest level” output remains rejected, but as a
borderline creative near-miss. Its coherent gaming system is desirable; the
specific verb introduces an unsupported prior death or failure.

## Recommendation

Advance Prompt v6 as the leading candidate, but do not promote it to the
default yet. Preserve v5.1 as the stable baseline and keep the current v6
outputs immutable.

The product-owner review of all initially rejected v6 translations is now
complete. Before another prompt revision:

1. Retain the transformation floor, high-leverage targeting, transparent
   coinage, and coherent metaphorical systems.
2. Tighten only the undisputed failures: changed central facts, genuinely
   unnatural wording, explanation rationalization, and repeated filler.
3. Treat recoverable, tween-plausible grammatical strain as permissible rather
   than automatically unnatural.
4. Validate minimally revised v6 on a fresh held-out set.

Do not add a phrase bank or tune against individual held-out outputs.

## Next Experiments

1. Make the smallest prompt revision supported by the twelve remaining
   product-owner-rejected translations.
2. Create a fresh held-out set for an unbiased v5.1-versus-v6 comparison.
3. Reuse this suite as a regression set.
