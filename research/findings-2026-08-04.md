# Evaluation Findings — 2026-08-04

## Evaluation Summary

**Prompt versions:** v5.1 and latest v6 semantic revision
**Dataset:** 15 fresh Standard English → Gen Alpha inputs
**Categories:** idioms, speech acts, factual/technical language, everyday
language, and contextual creativity
**Runs:** Three per input and prompt; 90 total
**Request integrity:** 90 successful responses; zero prompt-header mismatches
**Rater:** AI product review using the frozen launch rubric; prompt labels were
visible during final rating

The product owner independently reviewed an initial portion of the raw results
and observed that v6 was stronger on idiom resolution, creativity, and
explanation spans. Those observations are consistent with the completed
evaluation.

## Overall Result

Prompt v6 materially outperforms v5.1 and should become the launch candidate.
It improves translation approval by 20 percentage points and explanation
alignment by nine points without producing a category-level translation
regression.

| Measure | v5.1 | v6 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Translation approval | 30/45 (67%) | 39/45 (87%) | +20 points |
| Explanation alignment | 38/45 (84%) | 42/45 (93%) | +9 points |
| Not transformed enough | 13 | 0 | −13 |
| Meaning changed | 1 | 1 | — |
| Unnatural or forced | 1 | 4 | +3 |
| Culturally weak or dated | 0 | 1 | +1 |

## Key Findings

### 1. Semantic interpretation fixes the central v5.1 weakness

V6 consistently translates the intended meaning of idioms rather than merely
casualizing or retaining their literal wording:

- Premature confidence becomes “pre-celebrate the W before it actually
  unlocks” or “a W that hasn’t spawned yet.”
- Starting a complicated problem becomes “spawned a whole chaos side quest” or
  “spawned a whole drama DLC.”
- Assessing a social context becomes “vibe-check the room” before introducing
  or “spawning” a topic.

V5.1 leaves one idiom unchanged and produces several ordinary or weak recasts.
V6 passes eight of nine idiom outputs; its only failure, “a whole drama spawn,”
is a localized naturalness problem rather than failed interpretation.

### 2. V6 produces stronger high-leverage transformations

Successful v6 outputs transform the sentence around one coherent idea instead
of attaching slang to an ordinary paraphrase:

- “Can you nerf the music while I clutch this call?”
- “My headphones straight-up despawned...”
- “The tiny café ... pancake-maxxes...”
- “The final slide lore-dropped and flipped the whole argument.”

V5.1 remains usable on straightforward everyday language, but its thirteen
under-translation failures show that it frequently stops at casual Standard
English.

### 3. Speech-act handling generalizes

V6 preserves gratitude, boundaries, requests, apologies, and denials across
eight of nine approved pragmatic outputs. The new semantic and attitude-holder
rules avoid the systematic appreciation drift found in the earlier regression
set.

The strongest outputs keep both interpersonal intent and target-register
energy, such as “Appreciate you checking my vibe, but I’m tryna solo-queue
tonight.”

### 4. Explanation quality improves alongside translation quality

V6 explanations are aligned in 42 of 45 runs. Spans generally isolate the
actual transformation, and reasons explain the cultural or metaphorical move
rather than quoting and paraphrasing the entire sentence.

The three failures are narrow: two combined approval-and-scoreboard changes
into spans that should have been split, and one explanation rationalized a
minor factual expansion from “crack” to “crack open.”

### 5. Technical language remains the principal launch limitation

V6 improves scheduled closure and vote-result translations, but all three
water-freezing outputs fail on awkward mechanical formations such as
“freeze-locks” and “crack-popped.” One run also changes a container that may
crack into one that cracks open.

This is not broad technical unreliability: exact time, repair reason, vote
tally, causality, and possibility are otherwise preserved. It is a recurring
naturalness problem when v6 tries to force coinage into scientific prose.

### 6. Gaming-language concentration is a known vocabulary limitation

V6 uses `spawn`, `spawned`, or `despawned` in 8 of 45 outputs (18%) across 5
of 15 inputs (33%); v5.1 does not use the family at all. Earlier v6 datasets
used it much less often, so the launch inputs partly contributed by repeatedly
involving appearance, initiation, introduction, disappearance, and existence.

The inputs do not fully explain the pattern. `Spawn` is also functioning as a
v6 vocabulary attractor: one versatile gaming metaphor is being reused for
several distinct semantic relationships. Some individual uses remain strong,
but their cumulative frequency weakens dataset-level vocabulary diversity.

Do not add a word-specific prohibition before launch. Monitor gaming-metaphor
concentration across a larger natural sample; if it persists, address it with
a general context-specificity rule rather than replacing one repeated term
with another.

### 7. The remaining failures do not justify more pre-launch prompt tuning

V6 has six translation failures: four unnatural constructions, one minor
meaning expansion, and one culturally mismatched TARDIS reference. None
recreates the earlier systematic under-translation, idiom literalization, or
speech-act failures.

Further tuning against this now-visible dataset would reduce confidence in
generalization. The technical limitation should be documented and monitored
rather than patched with input-specific prompt language before launch.

## Promotion Decision

The product owner approved Prompt v6 for promotion on 2026-08-04. V6 should
become the launch candidate and replace v5.1 as the application default.

The promotion is supported by:

1. A material aggregate translation gain
2. No category-level translation regression
3. Large improvements in idiom resolution and transformation strength
4. Improved explanation alignment
5. Remaining failures that are narrow, legible, and acceptable for an MVP

Do not modify v6 again before promotion. Freeze the evaluated prompt and model
configuration, then verify it through the release-candidate smoke suite.

## Launch Follow-Up

1. Freeze and tag the promoted prompt/model configuration.
2. Include unseen scientific or factual inputs in post-launch monitoring.
3. Track gaming-metaphor concentration across natural usage.
4. Treat this dataset as a regression set, not a future tuning set.
