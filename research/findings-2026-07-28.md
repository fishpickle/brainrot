# Evaluation Findings — 2026-07-28

## Evaluation Summary

**Prompt version:** v2

**Model:** GPT-5.6 Terra

**Evaluation set:** Standard→Gen Alpha intensity triplets and repeated
Gen Alpha→Standard translations

**Date:** 2026-07-28

### Overall assessment

Prompt v2 establishes a strong fidelity baseline and materially improves
contextual vocabulary use. Reverse translation is reliable for clear inputs,
while forward translation remains inconsistent at Mild and Cooked. The next
iteration should use direction-specific changes: sharper forward intensity
contracts and greater restraint around ambiguity in reverse translation.

## Key Findings

### 1. Intensity should not apply to reverse translation

Clear Gen Alpha inputs produced the same accurate Standard English result at
every intensity. This consistency is desirable because reverse translation has
one canonical goal. Showing Mild, Cooked, and Ohio Max in this direction creates
a false expectation that the outputs should differ.

### 2. Reverse translation is strong except when context is ambiguous

The prompt reliably recovered the meaning of clear phrases such as “no cap,”
“lowkey sus,” and “left no crumbs.” Ambiguous fragments were less stable. The
model sometimes introduced a subject, addressee, or emotional judgment that the
source did not establish.

### 3. Mild and Cooked still under-translate

Many forward outputs preserved meaning but remained casual Standard English
rather than feeling translated. Cooked frequently differed from Mild by only a
contraction, generic marker, or appended sentence. Ohio Max delivered most of
the distinctly transformed results.

### 4. Prompt v2 reduced filler but did not eliminate decorative changes

“No cap” appears less automatically than in v1, and several outputs use
context-specific language successfully. However, some translations still rely
on additions such as “y’all,” “no cap,” or a second explanatory sentence rather
than transforming the original phrasing.

### 5. Speech act and interpersonal stance need stronger protection

Across both directions, weak outputs preserved the topic while changing an
apology, request, skeptical remark, implied subject, or positive/negative
judgment. The fidelity rules need to protect these pragmatic properties
explicitly.

## Supporting Evidence

- “No cap that test was free” consistently became “Honestly, that test was
  easy” across all intensities.
- “That’s lowkey sus” consistently became “That’s kind of suspicious.”
- Repeated “She ate and left no crumbs” translations varied naturally in
  wording while preserving emphatic praise.
- “W rizz” became both “Great charm” and “You’ve got great game”; the latter
  introduced an unsupported subject and addressee.
- “That’s giving main character energy” shifted between praise, neutral
  description, and criticism, demonstrating unresolved contextual ambiguity.
- Mild forward outputs such as “Dinner’s ready” and “I gotta study for my
  chemistry exam” were faithful but did not feel meaningfully translated.
- Mild and Cooked produced identical room-cleaning outputs, while multiple
  chemistry outputs showed only small differences.
- “Could you turn the music down a bit? It’s kinda loud” added an assertion
  instead of translating the request in place.
- Strong forward results included “Dinner just dropped,” “the cell’s power-up,”
  “get my room un-cooked,” and “the whole sitch.” These changes were contextual,
  recoverable, and structurally integrated.

## Recommended Prompt Changes

### 1. Add an explicit reverse-translation ambiguity policy

Do not invent a speaker, subject, addressee, pronoun, or emotional valence.
Preserve a fragment as a fragment when context is missing. Prefer a neutral
rendering when praise versus criticism cannot be resolved, and use the
explanation to surface genuine ambiguity.

### 2. Strengthen the Cooked transformation contract

Require an idiomatic, rhythmic, or structural recast when the source supports
one. Appended slang and extra explanatory sentences should not satisfy the
contract. Cooked should be clearly transformed while remaining more accessible
than Ohio Max.

### 3. Resolve the Mild contract

Test a version of Mild that remains restrained but always contains one
perceptible target-register transformation. If users continue to reject it as
under-translated, remove Mild and simplify the forward experience to two modes.

### 4. Protect pragmatic invariants

Explicitly preserve the grammatical subject, speaker and addressee, request
versus assertion, explanation versus apology, praise versus criticism, and
certainty versus ambiguity.

### 5. Preserve successful v2 behavior

Keep the current handling of clear reverse slang and the preference for
context-specific forward phrasing. Avoid a broad rewrite that makes accurate
reverse outputs more literal or verbose.

## Recommended UX Changes

- Hide the intensity selector for Gen Alpha→Standard translation and describe
  the result as natural Standard English.
- Clarify the observable behavior of each remaining forward intensity.
- Decide whether “Gen Alpha” means strictly generation-specific slang or the
  broader contemporary youth and internet vernacular associated with it.
- Use “Why these changes” to explain genuine ambiguity and meaningful idioms,
  not trivial substitutions.

## Next Experiments

1. Compare v2 with a targeted revision using three separate suites: forward
   intensity triplets, clear reverse translations, and ambiguous reverse
   translations.
2. Test ambiguous phrases both alone and inside disambiguating context to
   measure whether the model uses context without inventing it.
3. Run each configuration multiple times and distinguish semantic stability,
   harmless wording variation, and interpretive instability.
4. Score forward results on intensity differentiation and authenticity; score
   reverse results on meaning recovery, pragmatic force, and ambiguity
   preservation.
5. Test a stronger Mild contract once before deciding whether to remove it.
6. Add requests, apologies, sarcasm, negation, formality, multi-sentence inputs,
   and inputs with unclear subjects or targets.

## Success Criteria for Next Iteration

The next prompt revision will be considered successful if:

- Reverse translation remains stable for clear phrases and does not invent
  subjects, addressees, or polarity for ambiguous phrases.
- Reverse translation no longer presents intensity as a meaningful control.
- Cooked is consistently and materially distinguishable from Mild.
- Mild either feels perceptibly translated or is removed from the product.
- Forward outputs transform the source in place instead of adding decorative
  slang or unsupported sentences.
- Meaning, speech act, interpersonal stance, and ambiguity remain intact.
