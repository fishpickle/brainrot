# Evaluation Findings — 2026-07-29

## Evaluation Summary

**Prompt versions:** v3 and v4

**Model:** GPT-5.6 Terra

**Evaluation set:** Browser-collected Standard→Gen Alpha and
Gen Alpha→Standard translations, including repeated inputs, technical language,
long-form inputs, and selected round trips

**Date:** 2026-07-29–2026-07-30

### Overall assessment

Prompt v3 successfully replaces the three forward intensities with one
canonical translation contract. It produces substantially stronger,
context-specific forward transformations while preserving meaning and tone in
most cases. Prompt v4 consolidates that contract into a materially shorter
prompt without evidence of a broad quality regression. Its forward translations
remain strong, but explanation spans are often too broad and do not isolate the
specific change being explained.

The next iteration should preserve Prompt v4's translation behavior and make a
narrow, separately versioned change to explanation alignment. Reverse
translation, technical language, and matched v3-versus-v4 performance still
need more controlled evaluation.

## Key Findings

### 1. One canonical forward contract is more successful than intensity levels

Prompt v3 consistently produces outputs that feel translated rather than merely
casualized. Strong results use idiomatic recasting, compression, and integrated
target-register phrasing without depending on an intensity control. Removing
Mild, Cooked, and Ohio Max resolved an unclear product contract and avoided
making the former underpowered Cooked behavior the default.

### 2. Prompt v4 preserves the strongest Prompt v3 behavior

Prompt v3 received 28 positive ratings across 31 records; Prompt v4 received 21
positive ratings across 24 records. These rates are not directly comparable
because the datasets differ and v4 contains no reverse translations, but both
sets show a high proportion of useful forward outputs. Consolidation did not
produce an obvious collapse in transformation strength, fidelity, or vocabulary
variety.

### 3. Technical inputs expose the remaining forward-contract boundary

Both versions are strongest when the source supports a natural contextual
recast. Technical statements are less reliable: the model may under-translate
them, append an elaborate metaphor, or trade precision for energy. Modest,
recoverable verb-level recasts generally perform better than either ordinary
paraphrases or extended analogies.

### 4. Reverse translation is reliable for clear slang but not invented idioms

Prompt v3 accurately reversed established or contextually clear expressions
such as “dip,” “hyped,” and “low-key.” It was less successful when a forward
translation coined a novel metaphor. “In the void” remained untranslated, and
“dropped and cleared” was interpreted as release language rather than a
completed database migration. Round-trip failure can therefore reveal a
forward output that is creative but not sufficiently recoverable.

### 5. Explanation reasoning is usually sound, but span selection is not

Prompt v4 generated 31 explanations. Five quote the complete original and
translation, ten use an original span covering at least 75% of the input, and
fourteen cover at least half. Short inputs partly inflate these proportions,
but many explanations clearly discuss only one phrase while their
`original` and `translated` fields contain the entire sentence.

The problem is explanation traceability, not generally explanation reasoning.
Users can reasonably approve a strong translation even when its explanation
selects overly broad evidence.

### 6. Prompt efficiency is currently a maintainability concern, not a cost risk

The usage snapshot records 129 requests, 59,763 tokens, and $0.28 in spend
across multiple prompt versions and evaluations. It does not isolate v3 or v4,
but it shows no immediate cost constraint. Prompt v4 reduces the prompt module
by roughly one-third while preserving the five-explanation allowance and the
canonical behavioral obligations. Structured output remains worthwhile for
validated, predictable rendering.

## Supporting Evidence

- Prompt v3 produced strong contextual transformations including “lock in for
  my chemistry exam,” “get my room outta flop era,” “tap in,” and “We gotta dip
  in ten.”
- Prompt v3 preserved a long, emotionally careful message while sustaining the
  target register without making it more confrontational.
- Prompt v3 reverse translations recovered “DMs,” “tap in,” “hit me back,”
  “hyped,” “low-key,” and “dip” accurately.
- “My mom got me on room-cleaning duty” was rated negatively because it remained
  ordinary casual English rather than feeling unmistakably translated.
- “Don’t leave your backpack in the void” was approved forward but failed to
  produce a useful reverse translation, showing that recoverability must be
  evaluated separately from immediate appeal.
- Prompt v4 produced strong transformations such as “Dinner drops in ten,”
  “get your room un-chaosed,” “the meeting kept yapping,” “biggest W days,” and
  “Gravity basically yoinks objects back toward Earth.”
- Two Prompt v4 photosynthesis outputs were rejected: one appended a plant
  battery analogy, while the other remained an ordinary scientific paraphrase.
  “Basically flips sunlight into chemical energy” was approved as a smaller,
  clearer recast.
- For “Finish your vegetables before dessert,” Prompt v4 selected the complete
  sentences as its explanation spans even though the reason discussed only
  “dessert drops.”
- Repeated phrases such as “pull up,” “ghost,” and “drops” mostly occurred
  across repeated or semantically related inputs. The current evidence does not
  indicate broad phrasebook collapse.

## Recommended Prompt Changes

### 1. Add a minimum-span explanation contract

Require each `original` and `translated` value to contain the shortest
corresponding phrases needed to identify the specific change being explained.
Unchanged surrounding context should be excluded.

### 2. Align one explanation with one transformation

Split independently meaningful changes into separate explanation items. Permit
a complete-sentence pair only when the translation is a genuinely indivisible
holistic recast. Each reason must explain the full selected phrase pair.

### 3. Preserve Prompt v4's translation contract

Do not broadly rewrite or intensify the forward instructions. Keep the canonical
transformation requirement, fidelity safeguards, ambiguity policy, contextual
vocabulary preference, five-explanation maximum, and structured output.

### 4. Refine technical-language guidance only after focused evaluation

Prefer a restrained, recoverable transformation of a verb or relationship when
technical terminology must remain intact. Do not require a metaphor when none
improves the sentence, and do not let an appended analogy substitute for an
integrated translation.

### 5. Preserve Prompt v3 and v4 as separate controls

Treat the explanation revision as a new prompt version rather than overwriting
v4. Prompt changes can alter model behavior even when they appear editorial,
and preserved artifacts are necessary for controlled comparison.

## Recommended UX Changes

- Keep the intensity-free interface and the direction-specific “Cook it” and
  “Uncook it” calls to action.
- Continue showing “Why these changes” in the same result rather than adding a
  second API call or reducing the explanation allowance.
- Consider visually pairing or highlighting the exact source and translated
  spans once minimum-span behavior is reliable.
- Separate translation quality from explanation quality during evaluation.
  A positive overall rating should not conceal a broad or misaligned
  explanation span.
- Keep browser-only feedback as a development evaluation tool rather than a
  production analytics feature.

## Next Experiments

1. Compare v3 and v4 on the same fixed inputs, directions, and repeated runs.
   Score fidelity, naturalness, register transformation, vocabulary relevance,
   and explanation alignment separately.
2. Create a narrowly revised prompt that changes only explanation selection.
   Compare full-sentence frequency, span precision, and reason-to-span
   alignment against v4 while requiring translation quality to remain stable.
3. Run a dedicated v4 reverse suite containing clear slang, ambiguous
   fragments, invented forward metaphors, and longer contextual passages.
4. Expand the technical-language suite across science, software, medicine, and
   workplace language. Compare restrained verb-level recasts with more
   figurative outputs.
5. Add forward-and-back round trips as a diagnostic for recoverability, but do
   not require exact wording or treat round-trip similarity as the sole measure
   of forward quality.
6. Use held-out inputs that do not reproduce prompt calibration examples, and
   run each condition multiple times to distinguish stability from a favorable
   single generation.

## Success Criteria for Next Iteration

The next prompt revision will be considered successful if:

- Translation ratings remain at least as strong as the Prompt v4 baseline on a
  matched evaluation set.
- Explanations select the smallest meaningful aligned phrase in most cases.
- Each explanation reason accounts for its entire selected phrase pair.
- Complete-sentence spans appear only for genuinely holistic recasts.
- Technical outputs remain accurate and unmistakably translated without
  appended decorative analogies.
- Clear reverse translations remain natural, while ambiguity and invented
  context are not concealed.
- Vocabulary remains varied and context-specific without increasing slang
  density or emotional escalation.
